The Pacific Northwest doesn't hand you good surf. Resonant Swell is a geological forecasting model made specifically for its cold water, shifting sandbars, headland blockages, and unpredictable wind.

In Development

What it is

A forecast that understands the coastline, not just the swell.

Generic wave forecasts treat every coastline the same. Resonant Swell doesn't — it's a geological model built specifically for the breaks of Oregon and Washington, with headland shadow mapping, localized wind shelter analysis, bathymetry-informed wave shoaling, and tide calibration per break.

"We track how real swells actually arrive at real buoys, and calibrate our predictions against what genuinely happened."

What started as a forecast for Oregon's coast has expanded to Washington, with broader Pacific Northwest coverage planned as the model keeps learning from real conditions.

What's inside

Built from real ocean data, not a generic model.

🗻
Headland Shadow Mapping

Accounts for how headlands block, bend, and shelter incoming swell at each individual break — not just the open-ocean forecast.

💨
Localized Wind Shelter

Wind quality varies break to break. The model accounts for local shelter, not just a regional wind forecast.

🌊
Bathymetry-Informed Shoaling

Sandbars and seafloor shape change how swell breaks. Wave shoaling is modeled against real bathymetry, not assumed.

🕐
Per-Break Tide Calibration

Tide behavior is calibrated to each specific spot, since the same tide stage can mean very different conditions two breaks apart.

📡
Verified Against Real Buoys

Predictions are checked against NOAA NDBC buoy and CO-OPS tide data after the fact, and the model is calibrated against what actually happened.

Our philosophy

A genuinely local effort.

This isn't a big company — it's a small, local effort to build the forecast this coastline actually deserves. Rather than accepting mediocre forecasting alongside already-difficult conditions, Resonant Swell aims for something more honest: predictions grounded in the actual geology and behavior of Pacific Northwest breaks, continuously checked against what really happened in the water.
